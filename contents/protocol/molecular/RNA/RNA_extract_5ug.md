# <b>RNA extraction (&ge; 5 <i>&mu;</i>g)</b>

# Preparation

Usage amount per sample:

| Chemical                                        | Concentration  | Amount            | Location       |
| :---                                            | :---:          | ---:              | :---:          |
| Buffer RLT mixture <sup>&alpha;</sup>           | &minus;        | 450 <i>&mu;</i>L  | RNA bench      |
| Buffer RW1 <sup>&beta;</sup>                    | &minus;        | 700 <i>&mu;</i>L  | RNA bench      |
| Buffer RPE working solution <sup>&gamma;</sup>  | &minus;        | 1000 <i>&mu;</i>L | RNA bench      |
| DNAse &#8544; incubation mix <sup>&delta;</sup> | &minus;        | 80 <i>&mu;</i>L   | fresh-prepared |
| Ethanol (EtOH)                                  | &geq; 96%      | 225 <i>&mu;</i>L  | &#8548;        |
| RNase-free water                                | &minus;        | 30 <i>&mu;</i>L   | RNA bench      |
|||||

<sup>&alpha;</sup> **Buffer RLT mixture**: Add 10 <i>&mu;</i>L <i>&beta;</i>-mercaptoethanol ( <i>&beta;</i>-ME ) to 1 mL Buffer RLT. The mixture can be stored at room temperature for up to 1 month. Buffer RLT contains a high concentration of guanidine isothiocycanate, which supports the binding of RNA to the silica membrane. The <i>&beta;</i>-mercaptoethanol should be added to Buffer RLT to inactivate RNases.

<sup>&beta;</sup> **Buffer RW1** contains a guanidine salt, as well as ethanol, and is used as a stringent washing buffer that efficiently removes biomolecules such as carbohydrates, proteins, fatty acids etc., that are non-specifically bound to the silica membrane. At the same time, RNA molecules larger than 200 bases remain bound to the column.

<sup>&gamma;</sup> **Buffer RPE working solution**: Add 4 volumes of &geq; 96% ethanol to Buffer RPE. In this case, add 2000 &mu;L ethanol to 500 &mu;L Buffer RPE. It is a mild washing buffer for removing traces of salts, which are still on the column due to buffers used earlier in the protocol.

<sup>&delta;</sup> **DNase &#8544; incubation mix**: Add <b style='color: olive'>10 &mu;L DNase &#8544; stock solution</b> to <b style='color: olive'>70 &mu;L Buffer RDD</b>, mix by inverting the tube, and put on ice.

> - **DNase &#8544; stock solution**: Located at 4&deg;C (b). Dissolve the lyophilized DNase &#8544; in 550 &mu;L RNase-free water. To avoid loss of DNase &#8544;, do not open the vial. Inject RNase-free water into the vial using an RNase-free needle and syringe. Mix gently by inverting the vial. Do not vortex. Store the stock solution as single use aliquots at &minus;20&deg;C (9 months) or 4&deg;C (1 month). Do not refreeze after thawing.
> - **Buffer RDD**: Located at 4&deg;C (b). It provides efficient on-column digestion of DNA and also ensures that the RNA remains bound to the column.

# Procedure

All instruments must be sterilized prior to the experiment to ensure no RNA contamination and RNase residual.

## **Step 1: Cell lysis**

1. Put in 5 beads in each 2 mL microcentrifuge tube. Add <b style='color: darkorange'>450 &mu;L Buffer RLT mixture</b> ( contains 1% <i>&beta;</i>-ME ) into the eppendorf.

2. Dissect &leq; 100 mg tissue and immediately submerge them into the Buffer RLT mixture. 

> - The rice root tip weight is approximately: [ 17.7, 20.6, 19.7, 21.2, 22.3 ] mg cm<sup>-1</sup>/ 10 root-tips &approx; <b style='color: darkorange'>0.2 mg / mm</b>. So the maximum is 250 root tips ( cut 2 mm ).
> - Put the tissue into an eppendorf, slightly centrifuge to spin down the tissue to the bottom of the eppendorf. If the tissue is not used immediately, store the sample at &minus;80&deg;C.

3. Disrupt the tissue using Tissue Grinder ( <b style='color:darkorange'>30 hits / sec</b>, <b style='color:darkorange'>10 mins</b> ).

> - Place a thick layer of tissue paper between the tissue grinder adapter cover and the eppendorf tube caps to boost grinding efficiency and prevent the tube caps from popping open during high-speed reciprocal shaking.

4. (optional) Water bath ( <b style='color: red'>56&deg;C</b>, <b style='color: red'>1 ~ 3 mins</b> ). Help to disrupt the tissue and release RNA.

5. Now we get the <b style='color: darkorange'>raw lysate</b>.

## **Step 2: Collect the lysate supernatant**

1. Transfer the <b style='color: darkorange'>raw lysate</b> to a <b style='color: #896790'>QIAshredder spin column</b> ( purple lid ) placed in a <b>2 mL collection tube</b>. Centrifuge ( <b style='color: darkorange'>full speed</b>, <b style='color: darkorange'>2 mins</b> ). 

> - The QIAshredder spin column is used for removing cell debris and simultaneously homogenizes the lysate.

2. (optional) Transfer the supernatant to a <b style='color: purple'>gDNA Eliminator Mini Spin Column</b> (colorless, with purple O-ring) placed in a 2 mL collection tube (supplied). Centrifuge ( &ge; <b style='color: darkorange'>10,000 rpm</b> or 8000 &times; g, <b style='color: darkorange'>30 secs</b> ). Discard the column and save the flow-through.

> - The gDNA Eliminator Mini Spin Column is the component of RNeasy&reg; Plus Mini Kit. It is not sold separately.

3. Transfer the supernatant of the flow-through to a new 1.5 mL microcentrifuge tube (not supplied) without disturbing the remaining cell-debris pellet.

4. Add <b style='color: darkorange'>225 &mu;L 96~100% ethanol</b> ( &approx; 0.5 volume ) to the cleared lysate, and <b style='color: darkorange'>mix well</b> immediately by pipetting. <i><b style='color: red'>DO NOT centrifuge</b></i>. Proceed to the next step immediately.

> - The volume of lysate may be less than 450 &mu;L due to loss during homogenization.
> - Precipitates may be visible after additon of ethanol. This does not affect the procedure.

## **Step 3: Bind RNA to column**

1. Transfer the sample lysate ( usually 650 &mu;L ), including any precipitate that may have formed, to an <b style='color: violet'>RNeasy spin column</b> ( pink lid ) placed in a 2 mL collection tube (supplied). Close the lid gently.

2. Centrifuge ( &ge; <b style='color: darkorange'>10,000 rpm</b> or 8000 &times; g, <b style='color: darkorange'>15 secs</b> ). <b style='color: aqua'>Discard the flow-through</b>. Reuse the collection tube.

> - If the sample volume exceeds 700 &mu;L, centrifuge successive aliquots in the same RNeasy spin column. Discard the flow-through after each centrifugation.

## **Step 4: DNA digestion (optional)**

1. Add <b style='color: darkorange'>350 &mu;L Buffer RW1</b> to the <b style='color: violet'>RNeasy spin column</b>. Close the lid gently. 

2. (optional) Incubate on benchtop ( 25&deg;C, 3&ndash;5 mins ) before centrifuging to enhance removal of excess gDNA prior to applying the enzyme.

3. Centrifuge ( &ge; <b style='color: darkorange'>10,000 rpm</b> or 8000 &times; g, <b style='color: darkorange'>15 secs</b> ) to wash the <b style='color: violet'>RNeasy spin column membrane</b>. <b style='color: aqua'>Discard the flow-through</b>. Reuse the collection tube.

4. Add <b style='color: darkorange'>10 &mu;L DNase &#8544;</b> to <b style='color: darkorange'>70 &mu;L Buffer RDD</b>. Mix well by gently inverting the tube. Centrifuge briefly to collect residual liquid from the sides of the tube.

> - DNase &#8544; is especially sensitive to physical denaturation. Mixing should only be carried out by gently inverting the tube. Do not vortex.

5. Add the <b style='color: darkorange'>80 &mu;L DNase &#8544; incubation mix</b> directly to the <b style='color: violet'>RNeasy spin column membrane</b>, and place on the benchtop ( <b style='color: darkorange'>25 &pm; 5&deg;C</b>, <b style='color: darkorange'>15 mins</b> ).

> - Be sure to add the DNase &#8544; incubation mix directly to the RNeasy spin column membrane. DNase digestion will be incomplete of part of the mix sticks to the walls or the O-ring of the spin column.

## **Step 5: Wash**

1. Add <b style='color: darkorange'>350 &mu;L Buffer RW1</b> to the <b style='color: violet'>RNeasy spin column</b>. Close the lid gently. Centrifuge ( &ge; <b style='color: darkorange'>10,000 rpm</b> or 8000 &times; g, <b style='color: darkorange'>15 secs</b> ) to wash the spin column membrane. <b style='color: aqua'>Discard the flow-through</b>. Reuse the collection tube.

2. Add <b style='color: darkorange'>500 &mu;L Buffer RPE</b> to the <b style='color: violet'>RNeasy spin column</b>. Close the lid gently. Centrifuge ( &ge; <b style='color: darkorange'>10,000 rpm</b> or 8000 &times; g, <b style='color: darkorange'>15 secs</b> ) to wash the spin column membrane. <b style='color: aqua'>Discard the flow-through</b>. Reuse the collection tube.

3. Add <b style='color: darkorange'>500 &mu;L Buffer RPE</b> to the <b style='color: violet'>RNeasy spin column</b>. Close the lid gently. Centrifuge ( &ge; <b style='color: darkorange'>10,000 rpm</b> or 8000 &times; g, <b style='color: darkorange'>2 mins</b> ) to wash the spin column membrane. <b style='color: aquamarine'>Discard the collection tube with the flow-through</b>.

> - The long centrifugation dries the spin column membrane, ensuring that no ethanol is carried over during RNA elution. Residual ethanol may interfere with downstream reactions.
> - After centrifugation, carefully remove the RNeasy spin column from the collection tube so that the column does not contact the flow-through. Otherwise, carryover of ethanol will occur.

4. Place the <b style='color: violet'>RNeasy spin column</b> in a <b style='color: darkorange'>new 2 mL collection tube</b> ( supplied ). Close the lid gently. Centrifuge ( <b style='color: darkorange'>full speed</b>, <b style='color: darkorange'>1 ~ 5 mins</b> ).

> - Perform this step to eliminate any possible carryover of Buffer RPE, or if residual flow-through remains on the outside of the RNeasy spin column.

## **Step 6: Elute RNA**

1. Place the <b style='color: violet'>RNeasy spin column</b> in a <b style='color: darkorange'>new 1.5 mL collection tube</b> ( supplied ).

2. Add 30&ndash;50 &mu;L RNase-free water directly to the spin column membrane. Close the lid gently.

> - To increase RNA yield, incubate the spin column on the bench for 10 mins ( optional ).
> - If the expected RNA yield is &gt;30 &mu;g, repeat the elution step using another 30&ndash;50 &mu;L RNase-free water. That means, you will get 60&ndash;100 &mu;L eluate.

3. Centrifuge ( &ge; <b style='color: darkorange'>10,000 rpm</b> or 8000 &times; g, <b style='color: darkorange'>1 min</b> ) to elute the RNA.

> - If high RNA concentration is required, repeat the elution step using the eluate. The RNA yield will be 15&ndash;30% less than that obtained using a second volume of RNase-free water, but the final RNA concentration will be higher.

4. Store the RNA eluate at &minus;20&deg;C ( few weeks ) or &minus;80&deg;C ( > 1 year ).
